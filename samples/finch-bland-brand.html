Bland Brand?

April 4, 2012 · Writing by William Stone

We’re going to tell you something that few branding firms would admit—when it comes to direct marketing, often unbranded components generate greater immediate response than do branded ones. Does this mean that there’s no value in branding? Hardly. But we’ll get to that in just a moment.

The misleading reality of unbranded mailings sometimes outperforming branded ones has been a known fact among direct marketers for many years. It’s the reason why businesses occasionally send out correspondence in envelopes without any clear indicators as to their identity. Research shows that these envelopes sometimes get opened at higher rates than branded pieces.

That makes sense if you think about it. With a branded piece, our decision whether or not to open the envelope would be influenced by the brand—is it one that matters to us? With unbranded pieces, we have no choice but to open the envelope to identify the mailer.

The real issue here, however, is the difference between immediate results versus long-term ones. Of course, all companies share the goal of being successful. Occasionally this leads some to take marketing short-cuts and invest more significance in the micro view of campaign response rates than in the macro view of eventual sales-influencing brand building.

This fact was illustrated in a blog post published just yesterday about a banner ad test for EA’s “Need for Speed” video game franchise. Two versions of the ad, budgeted at 15,000 impressions each, were pitted against each other. One version branded the ad by prominently featuring the EA logo. It also included a nice image of actual in-game content, and a large call to action button promoting “free online racing”. The other version featured a rudimentary, almost childish unbranded design made in five minutes using Microsoft Paint. The results were 0.049% click-through rate (CTR) versus 0.137% CTR in favor of the latter version.

The tester interpreted this to mean that the unbranded piece was the more successful one. We disagree. Any direct marketer worth his or her weight knows that measurements of success should span several years, not just the weeks or months of an active campaign.

This is where the true value of branding comes in. Shortsighted companies ignore brand building in favor of activities that have the greatest impact on immediate sales. Engaging in marketing tactics that ignore brand, however, does little to help them strategically plot their course toward future growth. Branding is about the relationship an individual has with a company, product, or service. Relationship-building is a long-term investment. We’re talking about consumer preference, retention and future sales, not just lining one’s pockets today. Of course, a branded campaign’s outcome is driven by how successful the brand is in the first place. Perhaps the branded banner ad discussed above was less successful than its unbranded counterpart because of the perceived problems with the EA brand. In support of this theory, please consider an article in Forbes, also published yesterday, that covered EA’s race with Bank of America for The Consumerist’s award for “Worst Company in America”.

The bottom line is only through brand building can you become an integral part of your customer’s lives, so make sure your brand stands for something meaningful today so that come tomorrow you’re still standing.

Rebrand or Retire

April 19, 2012 · Writing by William Stone

When things are going well, people have a tendency to take their foot off of the gas and let inertia continue driving them forward. Business leaders by and large are no exception, so why rebrand when you have had a proven formula that worked? Open up the business section of any paper and the reasons should be obvious. Kodak is in bankruptcy. Instagram is worth $1 billion.

How did this happen? How did new-comer Instragram (launched October 2010) become such a hot commodity, while Kodak, a brand that had been synonymous with photos for most of its 123 year existence, lost so much ground?

Kodak’s brand dominance was such that for many years people widely adopted its tagline to refer to any event that begged recording for prosperity as a “Kodak moment.” It looked like the sun would never set on their photographic empire. The problem is, Kodak stopped being relevant to its consumers and squandered the 90% U.S. market share of photographic film sales that it enjoyed in in the mid-seventies.

Momentum is a funny thing. It only keeps you moving forward for so long before friction begins to slow your progress. Savvy business leaders give their brands a bit more gas before they ever decelerate. Refreshing your brand so that it remains relevant in a constantly changing world of consumer choice helps you keep the pedal to the metal.

Brand evolution is not just a “nice to have,” but an absolute requirement for business survival. When was the last time you took a look at your brand?
